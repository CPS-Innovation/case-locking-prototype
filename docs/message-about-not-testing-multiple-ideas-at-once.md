Caroline Jarrett sets out "why not" here:

https://www.effortmark.co.uk/designing-comparative-evaluations/

Here’s a brief summary:

- You won’t get a clear answer
- Your results will get contaminated
- You need a lot more participants
- Your big differences look identical to users

Also, it’s a lot more work for research and design, and in my experience, usually unnecessary.

Don’t get me wrong: if you/we/the team have multiple ideas, would love to hear about them.

But I think that what gives us confidence is first discussing them between us. Usually there is a clear winner (which might be the combination of multiple different initial ideas).

Then we do our best, test it and iterate. That’s what gives us confidence in the end.
